# Probability fundamentals: the twelve concepts. Order is a sensible teaching
# order; "requires" says what must be understood first. Format: gym/pack/FORMAT.md.

concept: sample_space
name: Sample spaces and equally likely outcomes
summary: List every possible outcome; when they are equally likely, a probability is favourable outcomes over total outcomes.
source: Classical definition (Laplace): for a finite sample space of equally likely outcomes, the probability of an event is the number of outcomes in the event divided by the number of outcomes in the sample space.
misconception: Treating outcomes as equally likely when they are not, such as the eleven possible sums of two dice.
misconception: Counting the outcomes of two dice as unordered, so that a 3 and a 4 looks like one outcome instead of two.

concept: complement
name: The complement rule
summary: The probability that an event does not happen is 1 minus the probability that it does, which makes "at least one" questions easy.
requires: sample_space
source: For any event A, P(not A) = 1 - P(A), because A and not-A are mutually exclusive and together cover the whole sample space, whose probability is 1.
misconception: Computing "at least one" by adding up every case instead of subtracting the "none" case from 1.
misconception: Thinking the complement of "at least one" is "exactly one" rather than "none".

concept: addition_rule
name: The addition rule
summary: P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B); the overlap is counted once, not twice.
requires: sample_space
source: For any two events A and B, P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B). When A and B cannot both happen the last term is zero.
misconception: Adding P(A) and P(B) without subtracting the overlap when the events can happen together.
misconception: Subtracting an overlap even when the events are mutually exclusive.

concept: independence
name: Independent events and the multiplication rule
summary: Events are independent when one does not change the probability of the other, and then P(A and B) = P(A) times P(B).
requires: sample_space
source: Events A and B are independent exactly when P(A and B) = P(A) P(B). Separate flips of a fair coin or rolls of a fair die are independent.
misconception: Multiplying probabilities of events that are not independent, such as two draws without replacement.
misconception: Treating "mutually exclusive" and "independent" as the same thing; exclusive events with positive probability are never independent.

concept: conditional
name: Conditional probability
summary: P(A given B) is the probability of A once we know B happened; shrink the sample space to B and recount.
requires: sample_space, independence
source: For P(B) > 0, P(A | B) = P(A and B) / P(B).
misconception: Dividing by P(A) instead of P(B).
misconception: Thinking P(A | B) and P(B | A) are the same number.

concept: bayes_rule
name: Bayes' rule
summary: Turn P(evidence given hypothesis) into P(hypothesis given evidence) using the prior and the total probability of the evidence.
requires: conditional, complement
source: P(A | B) = P(B | A) P(A) / P(B), where P(B) = P(B | A) P(A) + P(B | not A) P(not A).
misconception: Ignoring the prior (base rate) and reading the accuracy of a test as the probability that a positive result is right.
misconception: Confusing P(positive given disease) with P(disease given positive).

concept: expected_value
name: Expected value
summary: The long-run average of a random quantity: add up each value times its probability.
requires: sample_space
source: For a random variable X with values x_i and probabilities p_i, E[X] = sum of x_i p_i.
misconception: Averaging the possible values without weighting them by their probabilities.
misconception: Expecting the average to be a value that can actually occur on one trial.

concept: variance
name: Variance
summary: How spread out a random quantity is: the expected squared distance from its mean, E[X squared] minus E[X] squared.
requires: expected_value
source: Var(X) = E[(X - E[X])^2] = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2, and Var(aX + b) = a^2 Var(X).
misconception: Forgetting to square, so positive and negative deviations cancel.
misconception: Thinking Var(aX) is a times Var(X) instead of a squared times Var(X).

concept: counting
name: Counting arrangements and selections
summary: Count arrangements with factorials and selections with n choose k, then divide by the total number of equally likely outcomes.
requires: sample_space
source: There are n! orderings of n distinct objects, and n choose k = n! / (k! (n-k)!) ways to choose k of n objects when order does not matter.
misconception: Using n choose k when order matters, or ordered counts when it does not.
misconception: Forgetting that the number of ways to pick the other objects also counts, as in choosing 2 women and 1 man.

concept: linearity
name: Linearity of expectation
summary: The expected value of a sum is the sum of the expected values, even when the pieces are dependent; indicator variables make hard counts easy.
requires: expected_value
source: For any random variables X and Y and constants a and b, E[aX + bY] = a E[X] + b E[Y], with no independence assumption.
misconception: Believing E[X + Y] = E[X] + E[Y] needs independence.
misconception: Believing E[X Y] = E[X] E[Y] always holds, which does need independence.

concept: large_numbers
name: The law of large numbers
summary: Averages of many independent trials settle near the expected value, but the raw count of surplus successes does not shrink or get cancelled out.
requires: expected_value, independence
source: Weak law of large numbers: for independent identically distributed X_i with mean m, the sample average of n of them converges in probability to m as n grows.
misconception: Believing the law of large numbers makes the next trials compensate for an earlier surplus or deficit.
misconception: Believing the raw difference between heads and tails shrinks as the number of flips grows.

concept: fallacies
name: The gambler's fallacy and base-rate neglect
summary: Two classic errors: expecting independent trials to "balance out", and ignoring how rare something is when weighing evidence.
requires: independence, bayes_rule, large_numbers
source: Independent trials have no memory, so P(heads next | five heads so far) stays 1/2 for a fair coin; and a positive result for a rare condition is evidence weighed against its base rate, not a certainty.
misconception: Gambler's fallacy: believing a run of one outcome makes the opposite outcome "due".
misconception: Base-rate neglect: answering "how good is the test" when asked "how likely is it that I have the condition".
