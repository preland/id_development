item: space_1
concepts: sample_space
kind: recall
answer_kind: fraction
difficulty: 800
transfer: no
verified: tool
prompt: A fair six-sided die is rolled once. What is the probability of rolling a number greater than 4? Give a fraction.
answer: 1/3
rubric: Lists or counts the six equally likely outcomes.
rubric: Counts the outcomes greater than 4 and divides by the total.
hint_nudge: Begin by writing down every outcome one roll can have.
hint_question: Which of those outcomes count as "greater than 4", and how many outcomes are there altogether?
hint_step: The sample space has 6 equally likely outcomes, so the probability is (number of favourable outcomes) / 6. Count the favourable ones.
hint_example: The outcomes are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. Those greater than 4 are 5 and 6, which is 2 outcomes. So P = 2/6 = 1/3.

answer: A

hint_example: Sum 2 can only come from (1,1), but sum 7 comes from six pairs. The 11 sums are not equally likely, so 1/11 is wrong. The equally likely outcomes are the 36 pairs, and the correct answer is 6/36 = 1/6. The mistake is option A.

prompt: Three fair coins are flipped. What is the probability of getting at least one head? Give a fraction.
answer: 7/8
rubric: Identifies the complement of "at least one head" as "no heads".
rubric: Counts the 8 equally likely outcomes of three flips.
hint_nudge: "At least one" is awkward to count directly; look at the opposite event instead.
hint_question: What is the opposite of "at least one head", and how likely is it?
hint_step: P(at least one head) = 1 - P(no heads). Count the outcomes of three flips and how many of them have no heads at all.

item: complement_transfer
concepts: complement, independence
kind: solve
answer_kind: fraction
difficulty: 1200
transfer: yes
verified: tool
prompt: A machine has three independent parts. In a given hour each part fails with probability 1/10. What is the probability that at least one part fails in that hour? Give a fraction.
answer: 271/1000
rubric: Takes the complement: 1 minus the probability that no part fails.
rubric: Multiplies the three independent survival probabilities.
hint_nudge: "At least one fails" has a much simpler opposite.
hint_question: What is the probability that one particular part does not fail, and how do you combine that for three independent parts?
hint_step: P(at least one fails) = 1 - P(none fail). For independent parts, P(none fail) is the product of the three individual probabilities of not failing.
hint_example: P(a part works) = 9/10. All three work: (9/10)^3 = 729/1000. So P(at least one fails) = 1 - 729/1000 = 271/1000.

item: addition_1
concepts: addition_rule
kind: solve
answer_kind: fraction
difficulty: 900
transfer: no
verified: tool
prompt: A fair die is rolled. Let A be the event "the number is even" and B the event "the number is greater than 4". What is P(A or B)? Give a fraction.
answer: 2/3
rubric: Finds that the outcome 6 belongs to both events.
rubric: Applies P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B), or lists the union {2, 4, 5, 6}.
hint_nudge: Both events can happen on the same roll, so be careful not to count that roll twice.
hint_question: Which outcomes belong to A, which belong to B, and which belong to both?
hint_step: Use P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B). Find each of the three probabilities by counting outcomes out of 6.
hint_example: P(A) = 3/6 (2, 4, 6). P(B) = 2/6 (5, 6). P(A and B) = 1/6 (only 6). So P(A or B) = 3/6 + 2/6 - 1/6 = 4/6 = 2/3.

answer: A

hint_step: Compare the formula P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A and B) with the student's sum and see which term is missing.
hint_example: The king of hearts is both a heart and a king, so it is counted in the 13 and again in the 4. The correct value is 13/52 + 4/52 - 1/52 = 16/52 = 4/13. The mistake is option A.

item: addition_transfer
concepts: addition_rule, complement
kind: solve
answer_kind: percent
difficulty: 1100
transfer: yes
verified: tool
prompt: In a town, 60% of households own a car, 45% own a bicycle and 20% own neither. What percentage of households own both a car and a bicycle?
answer: 25%
rubric: Finds the percentage owning at least one as 100% minus the share owning neither.
rubric: Solves the addition rule for the overlap.
hint_nudge: Start from the households that own at least one of the two.
hint_question: If 20% own neither, what percentage own at least one? Then how does the addition rule connect that to 60%, 45% and the overlap?
hint_step: P(car or bike) = 1 - P(neither). Put that into P(car or bike) = P(car) + P(bike) - P(both) and solve for P(both).
hint_example: P(car or bike) = 100% - 20% = 80%. Then 80% = 60% + 45% - P(both), so P(both) = 105% - 80% = 25%.
