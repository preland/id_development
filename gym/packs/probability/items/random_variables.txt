item: expected_1
concepts: expected_value
kind: solve
answer_kind: decimal
tolerance: 0.001
difficulty: 800
transfer: no
verified: tool
prompt: You roll a fair die and win as many dollars as the number shown. What is your expected winnings per roll, in dollars? Give a decimal.
answer: 3.5
rubric: Weights each face by its probability of 1/6.
rubric: Adds the six products.
hint_nudge: Expected value weights each possible winning by how likely it is.
hint_question: What are the possible winnings and what is the probability of each?
hint_step: Add up (value) x (probability) over all six faces; the six probabilities are equal.
hint_example: Each face has probability 1/6, so E = (1 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 5 + 6) / 6 = 21/6 = 3.5 dollars.

item: variance_1
concepts: variance
kind: solve
answer_kind: fraction
difficulty: 900
transfer: no
verified: tool
prompt: X is 1 if a fair coin lands heads and 0 if it lands tails. What is Var(X)? Give a fraction.
answer: 1/4
rubric: Computes E[X] = 1/2 and E[X squared] = 1/2.
rubric: Subtracts the square of the mean.
hint_nudge: Use Var(X) = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2.
hint_question: What is E[X], and what is E[X^2] (the average of the squared values)?
hint_step: Find E[X] and E[X^2], then subtract the square of E[X] from E[X^2]. Squaring 0 or 1 changes nothing.
hint_example: E[X] = 1/2. X^2 takes the same values as X, so E[X^2] = 1/2. Var(X) = 1/2 - (1/2)^2 = 1/2 - 1/4 = 1/4.

item: variance_3

prompt: What is the variance of the number shown by one roll of a fair die? Give a fraction.
answer: 35/12
rubric: Computes E[X] from the six faces.
rubric: Computes E[X squared] from the six squares.
rubric: Subtracts and simplifies.
hint_nudge: You need E[X] and E[X^2]; the first is the familiar average roll.
hint_question: What is E[X], and what is the average of the squares 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36?
hint_step: E[X^2] is the sum of those six squares divided by 6, and E[X] is the sum of the faces divided by 6. Put them in Var = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2 and simplify.
hint_example: E[X] = 7/2. E[X^2] = 91/6. Var = 91/6 - 49/4 = 182/12 - 147/12 = 35/12.

item: variance_transfer

verified: tool
prompt: Daily sales are 100 units with probability 1/2 and 140 units with probability 1/2. What is the variance of daily sales? Give a whole number.
answer: 400
rubric: Finds the mean 120.
rubric: Averages the squared deviations of 20.
hint_nudge: Find the mean first and measure each value's distance from it.
hint_question: What is the mean, and what is the squared distance of each value from the mean?
hint_step: Var = E[(X - mean)^2]. Compute the mean, subtract it from each value, square the results and weight them by their probabilities.
hint_example: Mean = 120. Deviations: -20 and +20. Squared: 400 and 400. Var = (1/2)(400) + (1/2)(400) = 400.

transfer: no
verified: tool
prompt: In how many ways can you choose 2 students from a group of 5 to form a pair (the order within the pair does not matter)? Give a whole number.
answer: 10
rubric: Recognises that order does not matter.
rubric: Computes 5 choose 2.
hint_nudge: Order does not matter, so a pair counted as (A, B) and as (B, A) is the same pair.
hint_question: How many ordered pairs are there, and how many times is each unordered pair counted among them?
hint_step: Count the ordered pairs 5 x 4, then divide by the number of orderings of one pair. Or use "5 choose 2".
hint_example: There are 5 x 4 = 20 ordered pairs, and each pair appears twice, so 20 / 2 = 10. This is 5 choose 2 = 10.

item: counting_3
concepts: counting
kind: solve
answer_kind: fraction
difficulty: 1000
transfer: no
verified: tool
prompt: A fair coin is flipped 5 times. What is the probability of exactly 2 heads? Give a fraction in lowest terms.
answer: 5/16
rubric: Counts 2^5 equally likely sequences.
rubric: Counts the sequences with exactly 2 heads as 5 choose 2.
hint_nudge: Count the sequences with exactly 2 heads, then divide by the total number of sequences.
hint_question: How many equally likely sequences of 5 flips are there, and in how many ways can you choose which 2 of the 5 flips are heads?
hint_step: The number of sequences is 2^5. The number with exactly 2 heads is "5 choose 2". Divide and reduce.
hint_example: Total sequences: 2^5 = 32. Choose the positions of the 2 heads: 5 choose 2 = 10. So P = 10/32 = 5/16.

item: counting_4
concepts: counting
kind: solve
answer_kind: fraction
difficulty: 1150
transfer: no
verified: tool
prompt: A committee of 3 people is chosen at random from 4 men and 6 women. What is the probability that the committee has exactly 2 women? Give a fraction in lowest terms.
answer: 1/2
rubric: Counts all committees as 10 choose 3.
rubric: Multiplies the ways to choose the women and the ways to choose the man.
hint_nudge: All committees of 3 are equally likely; count the ones with exactly 2 women.
hint_question: How many committees of 3 can be formed from 10 people, and in how many ways can you pick 2 women and then 1 man?
hint_step: P = [ (ways to choose 2 of the 6 women) x (ways to choose 1 of the 4 men) ] / (ways to choose 3 of the 10 people).
hint_example: 10 choose 3 = 120 committees. 6 choose 2 = 15 and 4 choose 1 = 4, so 15 x 4 = 60 are favourable. So P = 60/120 = 1/2.

item: counting_transfer
concepts: counting
kind: solve
answer_kind: fraction
difficulty: 1250
transfer: yes
verified: tool
prompt: A box holds 8 chips, 3 of which are defective. An inspector picks 3 chips at random without replacement. What is the probability that exactly one of the chosen chips is defective? Give a fraction in lowest terms.
answer: 15/28
rubric: Counts all sets of 3 chips as 8 choose 3.
rubric: Counts 1 defective of 3 times 2 good of 5.
hint_nudge: Every set of 3 chips is equally likely; count the sets with exactly one defective chip.
hint_question: How many sets of 3 chips can you pick from 8, and in how many ways can you pick 1 of the 3 defective chips and 2 of the 5 good ones?
hint_step: P = [ (3 choose 1) x (5 choose 2) ] / (8 choose 3). Evaluate each binomial coefficient and simplify.
hint_example: 8 choose 3 = 56. 3 choose 1 = 3 and 5 choose 2 = 10, so 30 sets are favourable. So P = 30/56 = 15/28.
